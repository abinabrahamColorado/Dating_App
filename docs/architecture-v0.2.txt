Part 1 - Executive assessment
Go Mode is a plausible coordination advantage, not yet a defensible company. It reduces the
mismatch between people who are theoretically dateable and people who can actually meet now. It does not
create compatible local supply, establish trust, or predict chemistry. An incumbent could copy the visible
feature. A durable business would need unusually effective local recruitment, trusted operations, better
reciprocal allocation, and evidence of outcomes people value.

The strongest revised product is a finite connection service: a small number of mutually eligible
introductions, each tied to a feasible next step. Explore handles future meetings; Go handles short, explicitly
chosen windows of immediate availability. Shared event contexts can concentrate supply without pretending
that everybody at an event is available for dating.

Five decisions shape v0.2:

• Keep the boundary, revise the taxonomy. A preference can be learned; an explicit active exclusion
   remains binding until its owner changes it. Behavioral contradiction authorizes a prediction update, not a
   consent update.

• Replace proximity gradients with public rendezvous. No exact stranger locations, live distance
   bands, directional arrows to people, crossing histories, or counts of compatible people. Both users
   navigate to an agreed venue.

• Separate selection, experience, reliability, and safety. Interest is not chemistry; attendance is not
   integrity; a safety report is not a romantic dislike. Each has different labels, access rules, and
   consequences.

• Use overlapping candidate pools, not social castes. Geography and availability help retrieval.
   Identity groups and inferred appearance clusters must not become permanent membership boundaries.

• Prove coordination before personalization. Start with rules and auditable randomization; use learned
   models only when they beat a simpler baseline on calibrated, held-out outcomes and an online policy test.

The main founder assumptions I would reject are: finite pools automatically create commitment; private
photo embeddings eliminate racial discrimination; coarse distance guarantees privacy; verification makes a
person safe; more downstream behavior is always more truthful; and one million registrations necessarily
produce enough local training data.

Investment view: This is currently a service and marketplace execution thesis with a possible learning
advantage. It is not yet an ML moat. I would fund a bounded pilot with explicit continuation gates, not a
broad geospatial platform build.

All numeric caps, timeouts, retention periods, thresholds, sample ranges, and service objectives below are
proposed pilot settings, unless explicitly attributed to a source. They are not established scientific
constants or legal safe harbors.

Unifying primitive: Connection Opportunity. An opportunity is a time-bounded, consent-governed
invitation to a feasible social next step. Its type is PERSON, MEET_NOW, EVENT, or GROUP_ACTIVITY. Its
contract specifies eligibility, what information may be disclosed, what accepting means, expiration, capacity
consumption, and the next action. A person is not an item to acquire; an opportunity is a proposed
interaction.

Do not immediately force all types into one scalar score. An event click, a mutual introduction, and a date
are different actions with different denominators. Start with an explicit slate policy: at most a few person
opportunities plus one relevant event option. Learn type allocation only after outcomes are comparable and
attendance is not confused with romantic consent.

The product should create a finite attention window, not a finite social identity. Repeated voluntary group
participation can build familiarity. Repeatedly resurfacing someone who declined cannot.

Part 4 - Product philosophy
Introduction intelligence, human compatibility discovery. The service should increase the chance of a
feasible, wanted encounter and then step back. It should not claim to know a person's destiny, attachment
style, morality, fidelity, or social league.

Design commitments:

• Adult-only service; bilateral eligibility and revocable consent at every disclosure and meeting stage.
• No infinite feed, like inbox, public ranking, compatibility percentage, streak, paid exposure, or fabricated
   scarcity.

• A truthful empty state is a valid outcome. Use 'No available introduction right now' rather than implying
   the algorithm knows nobody is good enough for the user.

• Identity and enough context precede a meeting decision. Minimal profile information must not mean blind
   physical commitment.

• Matching feedback stays private. Separate actions control messaging, meeting, disclosure, and reporting.
• Location is a short-lived input for a specific purpose, not a historical user asset.
• No incentives tied to accepting, answering Yes after a date, staying online, or generating more dates.
• Accessibility, non-drinking venues, cost, transport, and permission-denied fallbacks are core logistics.
Gameful interaction means clear, calm feedback when a real action changes state: a single haptic when a
valid invitation arrives, a pleasant venue-selection animation, or a public activity route the user chooses.
Manipulative gamification means manufacturing uncertainty or reward schedules to drive checking.
Never animate a radar scan when no fresh inventory is being evaluated; never tell someone to move around
to unlock people.

Precedence: Exited/Restricted/Paused overrides all discovery; Focus overrides new romantic discovery;
capacity blocks new reservations; Ghost denies every nearby read/write. Server policy is authoritative.
Persist policy_epoch and mode_version; every nearby request, candidate read, invitation, and delivery
rechecks them.

Opportunity transitions: DRAFT -> RESERVED -> PROPOSED -> MUTUAL -> PLANNING or MEET_NOW ->
RENDEZVOUS_CONFIRMED -> MET_REPORTED -> CLOSED/CONTINUING. PASS, EXPIRE, CANCEL, BLOCK, and
RESTRICT can close at earlier stages. Attendance is a reported observation, not an automatic result of arrival
GPS. No means no for this pair; changing the radius or mode cannot reintroduce them.

For Go, every consequential transition is compare-and-swap on a versioned record, with idempotency key
and expiry. Both venue approvals must reference the same venue/time version. One person's Yes is
insufficient. Block or mode revocation wins over concurrent acceptance. A reservation is released exactly
once.

Reaching capacity is not consent revocation: acceptance of an existing offer uses its held slot and must not
fail because that reservation made the member full. New lookup stops while the allocated opportunity can
progress. Explicit Ghost still revokes every unaccepted nearby offer. Reservation-aware checks prevent the
last available slot from creating a self-canceling invitation.

Part 6 - Explore Mode
Explore is an asynchronous introduction service for people available over the next several days. Initial
hypothesis: a maximum of three active connections and up to three new introductions per week when slots
exist. These are caps, not promised deliveries or scientifically optimal values. Offer a lower personal cap; do
not punish slower replies or disability-related communication differences.

User flow: short onboarding -> finite introduction card -> private Accept/Pass -> mutual chat and schedule

Pipeline: (1) snapshot active status, intent and remaining capacity; (2) apply bilateral explicit constraints
and authoritative safety denials; (3) retrieve overlapping local/availability pools; (4) exclude closed pairs and
stale profiles; (5) check feasible shared time and both travel budgets; (6) construct time-valid pair features;
(7) estimate reciprocal willingness and meeting feasibility, or use rules; (8) allocate capacity with an
exploration branch; (9) reserve both slots atomically; (10) recheck eligibility and deliver privately; (11) log
exposure and policy; (12) collect stage-specific outcomes.

Availability should support both a concrete shared window and an explicit 'flexible this week' option. V0.1's
mandatory shared two-hour window can unnecessarily exclude shift workers and people seeking a short first
meeting. A feasibility estimate is useful; a default window length is not a consent constraint unless the
member chose it.

On an empty slate, say so without a rejection judgment. Offer the next honest batch time, a relevant public
event, or an optional wider travel/time setting. Broaden retrieval within existing permission automatically;
broaden a member's hard boundary only when they approve it. Never fill a quota with a pair that fails safety,
consent, or minimum feasibility.

Conversation capacity is not inferred solely from who has failed to reply. Reserve one slot for each
outstanding bilateral proposal, then count the resulting connection once. A time-limited soft reservation
prevents overbooking; delivered invitations also consume exposure budget. A plan inherits its connection
slot. Users can close stalled conversations privately; capacity rules must not pressure them to send
unwanted messages.

Initial timers to test: 48 hours to respond to Explore, a quiet reminder only if enabled, and an archive
suggestion after seven days without progress. Do not automatically record disinterest from expiry. Ended or
archived states must be distinguishable from deliberate rejection in training.

Part 10 - Cluster architecture
Use overlapping, expiring candidate pools. A user does not belong to one hidden social class. Candidate
membership is a query result over permitted geography, bilateral eligibility, time, transport, and optionally
an explicitly joined event. Do not cluster by race, caste, inferred ethnicity, attractiveness, wealth, employer
prestige, or a latent score that reproduces those boundaries.

Separate three objects: (1) a spatial index for fast retrieval, (2) a context pool such as a public event, and (3)
a finite presentation budget. A pool may contain hundreds of eligible users while the user sees only a few
introductions. That is a policy, not deception, provided the product explains curation and does not invent
scarcity.


Retrieval and expansion algorithm
1. Build eligible(u, v, t) from active status, blocks, explicit bilateral constraints, discovery scope, and
  current capacity. Access-sensitive exclusions belong here, not in learned ranking.

2. Retrieve local public-zone or declared-area neighbors and compatible time windows. For Go, require
  current session leases; for Explore, use non-live areas and future availability.

3. Form overlapping pools: local logistics pool, opted-in event pool, and an adjacent-area exploration pool
  within the member's existing travel permission. Do not require all soft interests to match.

4. Measure eligible edge count, predicted opportunity mass with uncertainty, recent coverage, and unseen
  candidates. Counts are internal; never expose small sensitive populations.

5. If local supply cannot sustain the next finite slate, expand geography/time within authorized boundaries.
  Keep a small bridge allocation even when local supply is adequate, to test retrieval blind spots.

6. Ask before changing hard distance, intention, age, or other active boundaries. Offer an event or an honest
  empty slate when no acceptable expansion exists.

7. Deduplicate across pools, exclude passed/blocked/closed pairs, and recompute before reservation. Do not
  show the same person through an event merely to circumvent a previous No.

Pilot default: retrieve up to 100-200 eligible neighbors per member where supply exists; this is a
computational bound, not a quality threshold. At small scale, score all feasible edges. Store candidate-source
tags only long enough to audit retrieval; membership itself expires with its context.

Tractable probabilistic design
For directional selection, begin with a pooled regularized logistic model:

logit p_select(u -> v | x, mode) = alpha_mode + b_u + beta_mode . x_uv + delta_u . x_uv

b_u ~ Normal(0, sigma_b^2) represents selectivity; delta_u ~ Normal(0, Tau) permits heavily shrunk
individual deviations only after evidence. Pair features are pre-decision values: permitted stated fit,
travel/time feasibility, a small set of optional common interests, profile presentation version, and current
context. Do not add a permanent candidate popularity intercept in MVP. Audit whether richer pair features
later reproduce a global hierarchy.

The prior for delta_u may reflect a stated flexible preference, but fit its scale on population data, not an
arbitrary 'importance 3 equals twenty swipes' rule. Use hierarchical partial pooling; learn each stage
independently at first. A long-lived per-user model is not warranted by three decisions or five dates.
Versioned batch fitting is easier to inspect than v0.1's approximate online Newton update.

For experience, start with a population categorical model of Yes/Unsure/No conditional on an attended date,
with missing feedback handled separately. Do not stack future feedback from the target encounter into its
own pre-introduction feature vector. Later history features must contain only earlier interactions and carry
timestamps. For durable outcomes, survival/competing-risk analysis is more appropriate than declaring 'no
relationship by day 30' a failure.

Drift: compare time-windowed validation and candidate half-lives such as 30/90/180 days; select decay by
predeclared out-of-time performance and stability, not narrative intuition. Until then, preserve timestamps
and use a modest bounded recent window. Changes in intention or availability trigger feature refresh and
invalidate incompatible opportunities; reset only relevant learned state, not blocks or safety records.
Consent never decays automatically.


Bias and logging
Observed behavior is conditional on eligibility, retrieval, display, decision, attendance, and feedback
participation. Propensity logging addresses only the parts actually randomized with known support. It cannot
recover chemistry for excluded pairs or eliminate unmeasured reasons people meet and respond. This
distinction follows the selection-bias problem formalized in recommender research. [M01]

Log candidate policy version, feature snapshot/version, retrieval source, decision history, placement, model
version, randomization branch, exact conditional choice probability, remaining capacity, and experiment
assignment. Do not log raw location, report text, or sensitive audit demographics in the learning stream.

marketplace counterfactual evaluation also faces capacity/interference effects. Use IPS/SNIPS or doubly
robust estimators only for supported estimands, with clipped-weight sensitivity, effective sample size, and
uncertainty; confirm policy changes online. A human changing a batch creates a separate logged policy with
unknown propensity, excluded from causal replay unless the intervention is modeled.

Part 15 - Reciprocal matching
Targets and correct probability factorization
Let X include only information available at invitation time. Let A and B be the two accept decisions; M an
attended meeting; Y a mutually positive continuation outcome. Then a valid chain is:

P(Y and M and A and B | X) = P(A | X) * P(B | A, X) * P(M | A, B, X) * P(Y | M, A, B, X)

This is a probability identity with correctly conditioned events, not a claim that each term can be reliably
learned. Blind simultaneous decisions prevent influence from seeing a Yes; they do not prove conditional
independence because unobserved shared context remains. Do not multiply separately trained
unconditional probabilities or double-count scheduling and attendance.

Prefer a pooled joint acceptance model when label volume supports it, trained on pair proposals with both
decision states and missingness distinguished. A four-outcome model for Yes/Yes, Yes/No, No/Yes, No/No can
capture dependence; undecided cases need a separate response process or censoring treatment. Until then,
a product of directional calibrated estimates is a transparent approximation with calibration checked at the
pair level. The founder's 0.90 x 0.03 versus 0.55 x 0.55 example illustrates reciprocal imbalance, but its
numeric joint probabilities require that assumption.

At launch, stated-fit arithmetic is a priority heuristic, not a probability. Human-reviewed logistics plus
eligibility and randomized ties are enough. Do not display or silently interpret a scaled 0-1 rule score as 80%
likelihood.

Use a lexicographic policy: eligibility and safety gates first, enforce capacity and coverage guardrails, then
maximize estimated wanted meetings. In the early pilot, meeting completion is the observable optimization
target while feedback is an outcome check. Once enough labels exist, target mutually wanted continuation
after meeting, with attendance as a constrained diagnostic. Do not permanently reward any first date with
arbitrary utility weights v1=1, v2=3.


Model contracts
All learned components must pass temporal and user-disjoint checks where relevant, compare to a base rate
and simple regularized baseline, and report Brier/log loss plus calibration by mode and meaningful
segments. Use bootstrap/clustered confidence intervals; AUC alone is insufficient.

Part 16 - Capacity/exposure fairness
Use a general graph maximum-weight b-matching formulation, not a forced men-versus-women
bipartite graph. Users may be eligible for overlapping sets across gender and orientation. A min-cost-flow
implementation is appropriate only for a genuinely bipartite subproblem; it is not a universal replacement for
general-graph b-matching.

For feasible edge e=(u,v), choose z_e in {0,1}. Maximize sum(z_e * value_e) subject to sum(e incident to
u) z_e <= free_slots_u, per-session/per-period exposure ceilings, one live proposal per pair, and explicit
safety/consent gates. Additional waiting-time priorities and soft exposure deficits promote coverage. A hard
minimum introduction for everyone can be infeasible; report unmet demand and use slack, never violate
consent to satisfy a floor.

MVP uses an auditable greedy allocator with waiting-time rotation and randomized ties/exploration.
Benchmark it against an exact small-graph solver offline to quantify lost allocation value. Move to a
general-graph solver when measured benefit exceeds complexity. Stable matching addresses stability under
assumed preferences, not automatically our objective, consent uncertainty, or capacity fairness. It is not the
default merely because dating sounds like marriage matching.

Attention accounting: bound both active connections and proposed exposures. A high-demand person
cannot receive hundreds of likes because there is no unilateral inbox and a proposal reserves both sides.
Each profile has an exposure budget independent of incoming demand. Close/reject releases capacity but
does not erase the exposure ledger or permit a new account to evade a block. Do not require replies to
harassment to unlock capacity.

Part 18 - Safe Zones / real-world meeting
Call these approved public meeting places, not guaranteed Safe Zones. A busy cafe can still host
harassment; a partner badge must not imply supervision or an emergency-response promise. Publish the
specific checks and when they were last reviewed.

For each venue maintain: public address and entrance, opening hours/timezone, live session eligibility,
accessible entrance/seating/toilets where verified, seating/no-purchase options, cost band, alcohol-free
options, staff contact and training status if partnered, transit access, complaint history, emergency exit
notes, and review expiry. Require human approval at launch. Do not treat map ratings, police statistics, or a
school's reputation as a safety certification.

Exclude private homes, hotel rooms, isolated trails, closed venues, sensitive service locations, and places
whose access conflicts with the member's needs. Libraries, student unions and malls require permitted
meeting areas and hours, not blanket approval of a building. Campus enrollment must not expose students
to staff power imbalances; university geofences can contain homes and medical facilities. Nightlife requires
explicit alcohol preference, staffed opening hours, and a non-drinking alternative.

Selection: first filter for both users' accessibility, time, budget, activity, and allowed public-zone constraints.
Then prefer low worst-case travel burden, using max(t_u(v), t_v(v)) and a secondary imbalance penalty.
Weights are policy choices to test, not romantic utility. Use approximate origins or on-device route
estimates; never transmit one member's origin to the other. Present a stable set of a few zone-level venues
rather than changing the midpoint as people move.

Both choose/approve the same venue and start-time version. Either may decline, suggest another approved
public option, or cancel without disclosing a personal reason. A venue closure invalidates the plan and
requires new bilateral confirmation; no silent rerouting. 'I'm here', 'Running late', and 'I need to leave' are
manual messages, not live ETA or routes. Chat and recognizable profile information are available before
travel.

An optional trusted-contact share contains only the member-selected venue, time and minimal plan details.
Use the OS share sheet or an explicitly addressed contact action; never upload an address book by default. A
trusted contact may also be an abuser, so sharing is optional, revocable where possible, and never required
to use Go. A future check-in service needs delivery monitoring, escalation rules, and explicit setup. Do not
copy v0.1's automatic 'no reply means alert someone' without operational support and false-alarm design.

After a no-show, offer a safe way to leave, support, and any promised fee relief. First check evidence and
circumstances; safety exits, transport failures and emergencies are not moral failures. Repeated
corroborated abuse of the service can restrict scheduling after review and appeal. No public reliability badge
or negative review of a person.

Part 19 - Event/community integration
Events are promising because they concentrate time, place and shared activity. They may also make first
contact less intense and permit voluntary familiarity. These are reasonable inferences, not proof that
event dating produces better relationships than one-to-one introductions.

Start with a few recurring, explicitly opt-in public activities run with known hosts. Examples: a cafe game
evening, accessible short walk with staffed gathering point, a class, or a volunteer social. Do not launch a
broad event marketplace. An event should have value without a romantic match, and non-dating
participants must not be added to a romantic pool.

Data flow: verified organizer creates event and public venue -> operator reviews time, consent language
and conduct plan -> member RSVPs -> separate dating-discovery opt-in -> check-in/attendance validation
-> temporary event candidate pool -> ordinary bilateral constraints and capacity -> private introduction ->
meeting/continuation -> membership expires. RSVP alone neither proves attendance nor authorizes
discovery. Event ending revokes Go presence, not a mutually created ordinary conversation.

Use event-specific authorization tokens with scope, expiry, and revocation. Organizers see operational
attendance only when necessary; they do not see sexuality, private matches, refusals, feedback, or
safety-case details. Do not publish a roster of who enabled Go. No romantic-discovery link to a member's
workplace, faith group, or alumni identity without explicit consent.

For Meetup/Eventbrite, MVP can link to reviewed listings or work directly with the organizer. Respect
platform terms and partner authorization. Do not scrape attendee identities, infer dating availability from
tickets, or assume current APIs permit attendee import. Deduplicate canceled/rescheduled events and
require re-consent if the dating scope changes.

Part 20 - Trust and safety
Safety is a service with accountable people, not a hidden model multiplier. Maintain separate identity
assurance, conduct integrity, operational reliability, and safety case management. A concern can trigger
immediate precautionary restrictions without declaring an allegation proven; communicate neutrally and
provide an appropriate appeal path.

Identity: verify contact ownership and adult eligibility; use proportionate photo/liveness/age checks before
high-risk features. Vendor checks need performance evidence, subgroup error review, appeal/manual
alternatives, retention contracts, and clear explanations. A verified badge means only the specific check
passed. It never means background-checked, harmless, truthful in all respects, or incapable of creating
another account.

Conduct: blocks are immediate and bilateral in discovery, with no notice identifying the blocker. Restrict
messaging after unmatch but preserve reporting and member access to relevant own records. Monitor
enumerating access patterns, repeated creation, abusive invitations, and corroborated integrity problems.
Risk flags should add friction or review proportionately, not automatically infer criminality or identity from
weak proxies.

Safety operations: staffed review during every live Go pilot window, published support hours, emergency
resources appropriate to launch jurisdiction, a trained incident lead, case severity definitions, evidence
controls, appeals and escalation. Do not promise 24/7 intervention if it is not staffed. Urgent imminent-harm
reports get immediate triage during staffed operations; nonurgent pilot goal is acknowledgement within 24
hours. These are staffing commitments to verify, not guaranteed protection.

Integrity versus reliability: an unverified mismatch can warrant clarification; repeated evidence of
deliberate material misrepresentation can warrant review. A late cancellation affects only logistics until
evidence suggests abuse. Missing reports or attendance history must not disadvantage new users. Reports
are neither public reviews nor features in a romantic model.

Safety tooling and ML: MVP uses transparent rules plus human review. For a bought content/identity
classifier, document its actual target, training/evaluation population, error types, appeal rate, and threshold
behavior; never assume a vendor's confidence is calibrated for this dating population. A future abuse model
would predict adjudicated policy violations in a specified window from permitted operational signals, not
'dangerous person'. It would require sufficiently representative adjudicated positive and negative cases,
precision/recall at review capacity, subgroup audits, and manual fallback. Do not auto-dismiss a new report
because such a model scores it low.

Contacts: v0.1's 'hashed contacts' are not automatically private: phone/email dictionaries are enumerable.
Prefer direct in-app block and manual exclusions initially. A future opt-in contact-exclusion system should
use a specialist private-set-intersection design or tightly controlled keyed tokens, strict rate limits, deletion,
and no positive match responses. Hashing alone is not anonymization, and a new account can still defeat a
known-contact block.

Kill switches: disable a venue, event, account, client version, new-person Go, or all Go independently. Keep
reporting and existing safety assistance available during shutdown. A reproducible post-revocation
disclosure, exploitable location oracle, or uncontained severe incident stops the affected feature
immediately. Restore only after verified remediation and operational review, not because engagement falls.

Part 23 - MVP recommendation
Phase 0: two to three weeks of a concierge service for roughly 50-100 opted-in adults in one realistically
connected local catchment. Use a secure operational tool, minimal profiles and availability, explicit
introductions, public venues, basic scheduling, voluntary feedback and staffed support. No precise location
collection. Manual matching validates demand and logistics but cannot establish the future algorithm's
causal advantage.

MVP: Explore, finite cards, consent/constraints, bilateral capacity reservation, chat/scheduling, public venue
roster, reporting/blocking, optional feedback, Focus/Pause/Exit, deletion, and versioned exposure logging.
Build event-bound Go as a feature-flagged prototype only after the base service works. Existing-match
meet-now is the smallest useful Go test; stranger introductions at a staffed event are a separate consented
step.

V1: repeatable local operations, calibrated pooled selection/logistics where data supports them, a better
allocation solver if it improves coverage, and bounded neighborhood Go after mobile/privacy validation. V2:
transferable population models, carefully validated individual residuals, multi-market learning with local
calibration, richer event formats. Long term: durable-outcome research and selective retrieval embeddings
only if the evidence justifies them.

Do not recruit a second market to disguise a weak first one. Do not make sophisticated ML a dependency for
launch. Do not make GPS permission a dependency for the entire product.

Data arithmetic, explicitly hypothetical: 1,000 active members each receiving three introductions per
week produce at most 1,500 pair introductions. If 25% become mutual and 50% of those meet, that is about
188 meetings. If 60% of individual attendees answer feedback, expected responses are about 225, not 375.
Under an additionally unrealistic independent-response assumption, only about 68 meetings yield both
responses. Counts are further reduced by capacity, eligibility and incomplete exposure. Plan sample budgets
using observed joint feedback rates, not a hopeful registration count.

At one meeting every two weeks, a member contributes only about 26 meeting experiences a year. That is

Power reality: a conventional two-arm independent comparison of 20% versus 25% outcome rates at 80%
power and two-sided 5% significance needs roughly 1,100 independent observations per arm. Dating pairs,
repeat users and event clusters are not independent; design effects and missing feedback increase
requirements. With few event clusters, a thousand individual responses do not create a thousand
randomization units. Treat the early pilot as feasibility and estimation with intervals, not a series of decisive
A/B wins.

Marketplace interference is real: assigning one person changes the options and capacity available to others.
Research shows experiment bias depends on market conditions and randomization design. [M02] Individual
randomization can be valid for isolated interface questions; it is not automatically valid for allocation policies.
Prefer market/event-session designs where enough independent units exist, estimate spillovers, and
predefine who belongs to the analysis population.

Switchbacks by week are not a cure: a match can persist into future weeks, depletion changes the pool, and
relationships do not wash out on schedule. For Go sessions, record repeat participants and carryover; use
cluster-robust inference and sensitivity checks. For lasting allocation changes, persistent randomized local
cohorts may be preferable if they do not split already sparse supply. If neither design is powered or credible,
say that evidence is observational.

Pre-register primary target, unit, horizon, practical threshold, segment guardrails, missing-data handling and
stopping criteria. Sequential safety stopping is mandatory; repeated efficacy peeking needs a planned
method. Report outcomes for all invited/assigned members, not only those who enabled location or
completed feedback. Survey both satisfaction and freedom to say No. Never run an experiment that
deliberately exposes someone to a known stalking channel.

Do not use 'active users' defined by app openings as the only denominator: success, disappointment and
algorithmic exclusion all affect activity. Report fixed entry cohorts, user-weeks voluntarily open to discovery,
and successful exits together. Safety guardrails include report accessibility and qualitative comfort, not just
incident counts. A pilot with no severe reported incidents is still too small to certify safety.

Part 27 - Business implications
The defensible core, if it exists, is local outcome delivery. It consists of trusted recruitment,
sufficiently connected local pools, repeatable venue/host operations, a reciprocal allocation policy that
serves more people well, and a consented, carefully measured outcome loop. Data becomes an advantage
only if it improves decisions and the benefit transfers; sensitive data volume itself is not a moat worth
accumulating.

If Hinge copied Go six months later, the toggle, map, venue arrows, timers, and most algorithms would be
easy to reproduce. Incumbent density could make its version work better. The startup must win a
constrained use case where it reliably creates meetings users prefer, at sustainable acquisition and support
costs. Do not claim that incumbents cannot change incentives or learn from dates.

V0.1 is right to examine incentive alignment but too categorical about pricing. Monthly subscription revenue
has a simple direct dependence on duration; referrals, reputation, lower dissatisfaction and acquisition
efficiency can counteract it. Pay-per-date rewards first-date volume and can also misalign with lasting
relationships. A fixed-term package can encourage under-service or repeated renewals. No pricing formula
automatically makes the company benevolent.

Test a transparent, non-renewing local service package or a small coordination fee with clear
credits/refunds, alongside ticketed hosted activities if operationally justified. No boosts, extra visibility,
hidden paywalls on safety, outcome-dependent pressure, or fees to report/cancel for safety. Never
guarantee a number of dates in a way that pressures someone to accept; a service guarantee can cover
scheduling effort or refund terms instead.

Illustrative unit economics, not market facts: if each participant pays $12 for a delivered meeting
service, revenue per pair is $24. If payment/support/verification allocation/venue subsidy and hosting cost
total $18 per pair, contribution is $6 per pair or $3 per participant. A participant attending three times
contributes only $9 before fixed costs and acquisition. At $25 CAC, that scenario is unviable even with a
popular product. Replace each input with actual pilot costs; include incident handling, refunds and founder
labor rather than pricing them at zero.

Venue partnerships should provide access, service or perks without buying recommendation rank. Disclose
commercial relationships. An expensive venue must not win over an accessible one because it pays. Keep
free/non-purchase meeting options where possible and measure whether fees disproportionately exclude
members with lower incomes.

Measure whether success brings voluntary referrals, but never require users to share relationship status
publicly or invite friends to unlock opportunities. The strongest retention outcome may be a user leaving.
Operating plans should distinguish happy exits, breaks, dissatisfaction and unknown exits rather than
treating all as churn to reverse.

Go's capital implication: citywide concurrency, mobile reliability and safety operations are expensive
before they generate revenue. Event-bound Go purchases concentrated supply with operations effort; it is
cheaper to test but not free to scale. Prove contribution and repeatable local activation before building broad
location infrastructure or expanding geographically.

Part 29 - What NOT to build
Do not build during the first 12 weeks:

• Citywide stranger radar, near/nearer meters, person directions, exact location sharing with dates, crossing
   histories, AR people markers, or counts of compatible people in a small area.

• Background movement history, home/work inference, contact-graph ranking, camera-roll ingestion, or a
   precise-location analytics warehouse.

• Race/caste/beauty clusters, global desirability ranks, leagues, visual-attraction scoring, or inferred
   sexuality/religion/personality/morality.

• A long onboarding battery, a chemistry percentage, public reviews, attendance badges, mandatory
   post-date surveys, or mandatory sharing with a trusted contact.

• Infinite feeds, like inboxes, streaks, loot-box mystery, refresh rewards, fake nearby signals, paid exposure,
   or a requirement to reply to unlock safety/access.

• A custom feature platform, two-tower retrieval, deep multimodal models, online Bayesian personalization,
   graph community detection, or many predictive models before labels exist.

• Open event marketplaces, venue booking integrations, live partner ETA, AI-authored romantic
   conversations, or an unsupported emergency-response service.

• A second city to mask local failures, broad paid acquisition before contribution is known, or outcome
   guarantees that pressure acceptance.

Do not remove useful friction merely because a model can automate it. Identity inspection, a question before
travel, bilateral plan confirmation and easy cancellation are product value. Do not add friction to report,
block, pause, leave, revoke location, or delete data.

Refuse the claim that any missing piece is solved by 'AI'. A model that cannot name its outcome,
observations, bias, uncertainty and baseline is not ready for the architecture.

A thin responsive app or one shared mobile codebase is enough for this pilot. Event-bound Go avoids making
background-native location the critical path. Build only the native location capability needed for a later,
approved neighborhood experiment, with actual device testing. Do not choose one OS without checking the
recruited cohort's access; exclusions caused by platform choice are part of the pilot's limitations.

Week-12 decision: continue if real local demand, feasible coverage, wanted meetings, manageable safety
operations and plausible contribution are present. Narrow to events if they carry the value. Keep Go
experimental if evidence is inconclusive. Stop or redesign if the product creates anxiety, cannot serve a
meaningful fraction of the recruited pool, or requires unaffordable acquisition/coordination. More features
are not the default response to weak demand.


Direct answers to the founder's final questions
Does Go fundamentally improve the company, or is it merely a compelling feature? Today it is a
compelling, unvalidated coordination feature. It could materially improve the company if it repeatedly
creates additional mutually wanted meetings with acceptable operational cost and privacy risk. Its broad
ambient version could also make the company worse by increasing fear, empty sessions and safety burden.
Event-bound Go is the appropriate test, not a commitment to the full concept.

What is defensible if Hinge copies it? Not the toggle, haptic, map, venue routing, embeddings, or
'designed to leave' story. The plausible defensible core is a trusted local network and operating system that
consistently turns reciprocal intent into good real-world experiences, together with a demonstrably useful,
consented outcome-learning policy. If that advantage does not show up in outcomes and economics, there is
no proven moat yet.

What would I refuse to build with your money? A people-tracking interface, a hidden racial/beauty
sorting system, a compatibility oracle, an engagement casino, or infrastructure justified by hypothetical
future scale. I would buy evidence through real introductions, good operations and careful measurement.
The first technical achievement should be that two people can freely say Yes, meet with less friction, say No
without cost or exposure, and leave the service when it has done its job.

Open questions to resolve with the pilot
The launch catchment, team capacity, operating budget, initial eligibility mix, support hours, event partners
and willingness to pay are not supplied. This report makes explicit planning assumptions rather than
inventing them. The strongest unresolved scientific/product questions are whether finite attention increases
good meetings; whether Go creates incremental value; whether recurring context improves outcomes
without pressure; and whether experience-informed learning beats simple feasibility and reciprocal-choice
baselines. Those questions determine the next investment, not the attractiveness of the demo.
